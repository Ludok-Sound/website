**The short answer:** if you need to move your library from one DJ program to another (Rekordbox, Serato, Traktor, Engine DJ, VirtualDJ or djay Pro) with its cue points and beatgrids, the tool is Lexicon DJ. If you want all your music on your own server, to listen to it on your phone and in your car, and to sort and prep it for your sets without paying a subscription, Ludok Sound. They do not fully compete: you can use them together.

## What Lexicon DJ is

[Lexicon](https://www.lexicondj.com/) is a library manager for DJs. It is a desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux, with a companion app for iPhone and Android. Its strength is conversion: it reads the library of one DJ program and moves it to another with playlists, cue points and beatgrids. It also cleans up tags, genres and artists, finds duplicates, generates cue points and offers cloud storage for your tracks.

It has a free plan that converts your whole library, and two monthly paid plans, Essential and Ultimate, for everything else.

## What Ludok Sound is

[Ludok Sound](../../) is a free, open source app under the MIT license, for Android (with Android Auto), Linux and Windows. It is the app for [SoundVault](https://github.com/Ludok-Sound/SoundVault), a server that runs on a Raspberry Pi, stores your music and analyzes it: BPM, key, energy and where the drop hits.

With it you listen to your library streamed or downloaded, and you sort and prep it for your sets: an inbox for new tracks, metadata review against Beatport, duplicates, audio quality, new releases from the artists you follow, harmonic mixing and visuals for the projector.

One thing to know before you try it: for now, the app interface is in Spanish.

## Where Lexicon DJ wins

- **Moving between DJ programs.** It is what it is built for: six programs, with playlists, smartlists, cue points and beatgrids. Ludok Sound only exports to Rekordbox and Mixxx, one way and without cue points.
- **Cue points and beatgrids.** Lexicon converts them, edits them and generates them for you. Ludok Sound does not touch them.
- **Mac and iPhone.** Ludok Sound, for now, only runs on Android, Linux and Windows.
- **Nothing to set up.** Lexicon installs and works. Ludok Sound needs a SoundVault server that is always on.
- **A product with a team behind it.** Lexicon is commercial software, in English, with priority support on Ultimate. Ludok Sound is a personal, open project, and its interface is in Spanish.

## Where Ludok Sound wins

- **Price and freedom.** Zero, forever, and the code is yours: you can read it, change it and take it wherever you want. There is no demo mode when you stop paying, because there is nothing to pay.
- **Your music at home, with no limit.** There is no cap on tracks in a cloud: the limit is your disk.
- **Real listening.** A full app on your phone, with streaming, offline downloads, crossfades, an equalizer and Android Auto.
- **The inbox.** Each new track plays from its drop and you send it to its genre with a swipe. Going through a pile of new tracks takes minutes.
- **Audio quality.** It finds the files that claim to be better than they sound, like a 128 kbps MP3 turned into a WAV, so you know what to buy again.
- **New releases from who you follow.** What the artists and labels you follow release on Beatport and upload to SoundCloud, ready to preview and decide.
- **Session visuals.** A projector window that draws each track from its key, its energy and its drop.

## What both of them do

Find duplicates, fill in tags and covers, clean up genres and artists, and suggest which track goes next. In Lexicon, duplicates and tag lookup are part of the Ultimate plan; in Ludok Sound everything is included, and no change is applied until you accept it.

## What each one costs

- **Ludok Sound:** nothing. What you pay for is the server (a Raspberry Pi and a disk, if you do not have them already) and the power it uses.
- **Lexicon DJ:** the Free plan converts your whole library. Essential adds track and playlist editing, the mobile app and 1,000 cloud tracks. Ultimate adds automatic cue points, duplicates, tag and cover lookup, the watch folder, a cloud backup of the database and 10,000 tracks. Plans are billed monthly, with a 30 day money-back guarantee, and their FAQ also mentions a lifetime package. Current prices are on [their pricing page](https://www.lexicondj.com/pricing).

## Can you use them together?

Yes. From the desktop app, Ludok Sound saves an XML with your playlists (genres, energy, years, favorites and your own), with key, BPM and energy, that Rekordbox imports. Once they are in Rekordbox, Lexicon can take them to the other programs.

A split that works: Ludok Sound to receive, sort and listen to your music anywhere; Lexicon for the last step into your DJ software, with cue points and beatgrids.

## Which one to choose?

- **Lexicon DJ**, if you play with several programs or are switching from one to another, if you work a lot with cue points, or if you use a Mac or an iPhone.
- **Ludok Sound**, if you want your music on your own server, to listen to it on your phone and in your car, and to sort what comes in without paying a subscription.
- **Both**, if you want the best of each.

## FAQ

### Is Ludok Sound a free alternative to Lexicon DJ?

Partly. It covers duplicates, tags, covers, genre cleanup and mixing suggestions without paying anything, but it does not convert between DJ programs or manage cue points and beatgrids.

### Does Lexicon DJ run on Linux?

Yes. According to their download page, on Ubuntu 22.04 or later and on Debian, 64 bit. So does Ludok Sound, as an AppImage.

### Do I need a Raspberry Pi to use Ludok Sound?

You need a SoundVault server that is always on, which is what stores and analyzes your music. It is designed for a Raspberry Pi.
